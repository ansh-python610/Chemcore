Quick definitions

High-frequency terms for revision.

Combination reaction

A reaction in which two or more substances combine to form a single product.

Decomposition reaction

A reaction in which one compound breaks down into two or more simpler substances.

Displacement reaction

A more reactive element displaces a less reactive element from its compound.

Double displacement

Two compounds exchange ions to form two new compounds, often producing a precipitate.

Oxidation

In Class 10 chemistry, oxidation can be described as addition of oxygen or removal of hydrogen.

Reduction

In Class 10 chemistry, reduction can be described as removal of oxygen or addition of hydrogen.

Core exam notes 📌

Definitions, observations and rules worth memorising.

How to identify reaction type

Combination: many → one product.
Decomposition: one → many products.
Displacement: A + BC → AC + B.
Double displacement: AB + CD → AD + CB.

Oxidation & reduction shortcut

Oxidation: gain of oxygen / loss of hydrogen.
Reduction: loss of oxygen / gain of hydrogen.
When both happen together, the reaction is a redox reaction.

Important colour memory

FeSO₄ → pale green
Cu → reddish-brown
CuO → black
Fe₂O₃ → reddish-brown
PbI₂ → yellow
AgCl → white → grey

Gas tests

CO₂: turns limewater milky.
H₂: gives a characteristic pop with a flame in a proper laboratory test.
O₂: supports combustion and relights a glowing splint.

Conditions matter

Write conditions above the reaction arrow: heat, sunlight, catalyst, concentrated H₂SO₄, electrolysis, etc. Missing a required condition can cost marks in equation-based questions.

State symbols

(s) solid   (l) liquid   (g) gas   (aq) aqueous solution. A downward arrow ↓ indicates a precipitate; ↑ indicates gas evolution.
